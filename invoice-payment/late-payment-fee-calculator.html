Understanding Late Payment Fees

Flat Fee Method

A flat percentage fee is the simplest approach — charge a fixed percentage of the invoice total regardless of how long it's been overdue. Common for freelancers and service businesses with straightforward billing.

Fee = Invoice × (Rate ÷ 100)

Monthly Interest Rate

Charge interest based on how many months (or partial months) the invoice is late. The US standard is 1.5% per month — equivalent to 18% annually. Interest grows with each passing month, incentivising faster payment.

Fee = Invoice × Rate × (Days ÷ 30)

Annual Interest Rate

Calculates interest using a per-day rate derived from an annual percentage. More precise for invoices that are only a few days late. Common in formal vendor agreements and government contracts.

Fee = Invoice × Rate × (Days ÷ 365)

Legal Considerations

Late fees must be disclosed in your contract or on your invoices before work begins — not added as a surprise after the fact. Some US states cap the maximum allowable interest rate on overdue invoices. Always confirm your rate is compliant with local law.

US Standard: 1.5%/month (18%/year)

Best practices for late payment policies

  • Put it in writing before you start. Your late fee policy must appear in your contract and on every invoice. A fee you add after the fact is unenforceable and damages the client relationship.
  • Use 1.5% per month as your default. This is the widely accepted US standard — high enough to motivate payment, low enough to remain legally defensible in most states.
  • Send a reminder before the due date. A brief "payment due in 3 days" message prevents most late payments before they happen. Reactive collection is harder than proactive reminders.
  • Apply fees consistently. If you waive late fees for some clients but not others, you undermine their purpose and create expectations of flexibility. Apply the policy consistently unless there's a genuine exceptional circumstance.
  • Know your state's limits. A handful of states impose caps on late fee interest rates. If you work with clients across multiple states, use a conservative rate like 1.5%/month that falls within limits everywhere.
