Understanding Overtime Costs

How Overtime Is Calculated

Under the US Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), eligible employees must receive at least 1.5× their regular rate for all hours over 40 in a workweek. Some states require overtime for daily hours over 8 as well.

OT Pay = OT Hours × (Wage × 1.5)

The True Cost of Overtime

Overtime doesn't just cost 50% more per hour — it also inflates payroll taxes (FICA) proportionally. An employee earning $20/hr costs $30/hr in overtime wages alone, before employer taxes on top of that.

Total OT Cost = OT Pay + FICA on OT Pay

When Is Double-Time Required?

California requires double-time (2×) for hours over 12 in a single workday and for all hours on the 7th consecutive workday. Some union contracts and industries also mandate double-time. Always check your state's specific rules.

Double-Time = Wage × 2.0

The Overtime Premium Explained

The "additional overtime cost" is the premium you pay above what you'd pay if those same hours were at regular rates. It's the true extra expense — the cost of working those hours in overtime rather than regular time.

Premium = OT Hours × Wage × (Multiplier − 1)

How to manage overtime costs without burning out your team

  • Track overtime weekly, not monthly. FLSA overtime is calculated per workweek. By the time you see high monthly overtime, the cost is already locked in. Weekly tracking lets you intervene before it escalates.
  • Compare hiring vs overtime cost. When overtime becomes chronic, a part-time hire or contractor may be cheaper than ongoing premium wages. Run the numbers — the breakeven point is often lower than managers expect.
  • Stagger shifts to avoid the 40-hour threshold. Scheduling 4 × 10-hour shifts instead of 5 × 8-hour shifts gives employees more flexibility while keeping all hours within the regular rate. Consult your state's daily overtime rules first.
  • Exempt vs non-exempt matters a lot. Salaried employees classified as exempt from FLSA overtime are not entitled to overtime pay regardless of hours worked — but misclassification carries significant legal and financial risk. Always verify classification with an employment attorney.
  • Factor FICA into overtime planning. Employer payroll taxes (7.65%) apply to all wages including overtime. A $300 overtime bill actually costs closer to $323 when you include employer FICA. This calculator shows the wage cost — add 7.65% for the complete picture.
